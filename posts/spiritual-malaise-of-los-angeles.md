*After the first few days being back in LA, here are a couple thoughts:*

---

**1. The Link Between Virtue & Aesthetics**

I am constantly struck by the connection between virtue (or lack thereof) and aesthetics/decorum (or lack thereof). The difference between Alberta and LA is stark in this department.

LA actually feels significantly less virtuous (especially noticeable in certain locations) than other cities I have been to, purely due to the visible signs of decay—think [broken window theory](https://grokipedia.com/page/Broken_windows_theory).

- **The Local Contrast:** Much of my neighborhood seems to be suburban and higher income, and was probably quite nice at one point.

- **The Decay:** Walk a few blocks, and the signs of decay come out in full force.

- **Comparison:** Edmonton had its fair share of broken windows, of course, but truly nothing compared to the visible signs of antisocial behavior present here.

This is especially noticeable because of the visible wealth present. I am within walking distance of a Ferrari dealership, which creates a stark contrast between the decay (graffiti and vandalism, cracked concrete, trash everywhere), and the wealth (bright red hypercars, ultra-high locked gates, expensive suburban homes). 

It feels as though I am living in a city with a **split personality**.

I suppose it could be a neighborhood thing, but I am skeptical. 

**2. Spiritual Heaviness & Malaise**

Alongside the aesthetic degradation, this place seems to be full of a spiritual heaviness/malaise.

I visited a small church near my house for their evening bible study, and was very refreshed to find a small contingent of brothers and sisters faithfully working away to bring the gospel to our area. To say that they are outnumbered would be an understatement. I pray for their success, because this place feels **spiritually oppressed**—as though the air is heavier than I am used to.

- **The Immediate Impact:** The result is that I find myself not wanting to explore as much as I usually would.

- **A Shift in Perspective:** I've said for the past few years that I didn't think places like Edmonton—or certain regions across Canada and the US—really needed someone like me. It's true that Edmonton has its own darkness and needs Christ as much as any other place, but after moving to LA, I fully see the distinction.

This place belongs on a list with the most despondent, dejected, and depraved cities of ancient memory—think Nineveh or Babylon.

It may be harsh to say, but only an abundantly merciful God could allow a place like this to continue—

**And only an abundantly powerful one could redeem it.**
