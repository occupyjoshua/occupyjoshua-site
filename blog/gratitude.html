Gratitude.

Feb 9, 2026

If there were ever one single practical tip I could give to people today, in order that they might live lives more satisfied and joyous, I would tell them to actively carve out moments in life to practice the virtue of gratitude. To recount even the smallest gifts they had been given, and to choose, in that moment, to be grateful for them. Unfortunately, in our consumer culture today, we no longer teach or encourage gratitude. Instead, as a collective, we focus incessantly on that which we do not have. This is why envy is one of the most silent soul killers, running rampant in the west today; gratitude and envy are opposites, if we fail to practice gratitude, it’s no wonder that envy abounds. Life becomes less about appreciating the incredible blessing each of us has, and more about eyeing hungrily that which someone else has. As I have said before, at a societal level, you get what you incentivize, and we are pouring social and financial incentives into the perpetuation of envy. A course correction is in order.

What do you think would happen as a society if our people took time every day to briefly acknowledge the good things in their lives? If they chose to recognize and be grateful for that which they already have? If instead of chasing the next high, they sat for a moment and enjoyed what was already in front of them? Gratitude is quite difficult, I understand. It requires a measure of discipline, like any virtue. But its benefits outweigh the sacrifice. Any cursory examination of the evidence will clearly demonstrate a tie between one’s ability to be grateful, and one’s overall life satisfaction. If hope is that virtue which brings with it the greatest ability to change the future and drive us towards a better tomorrow, gratitude is that virtue which brings with it the greatest ability to appreciate today. Gratitude allows us to rest and refocus ourselves on the things that matter, keeping us from getting lost in a constant push for something else. It reminds us that each moment is a gift, and forces us to slow down when everything else in life says ‘go faster’.

So how do we do this? I’m a bible thumper, so when I have questions as such, I look to scripture;

“give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you.”

I am not trying to sound trite when I say it really is that simple. In every circumstance in this life, there are opportunities to practice gratitude. Every circumstance. Sorry if that sounds too hardcore for you, but I mean it, and I try very hard (with varying levels of success) to implement it in my life, and have for many years now. I practice what I preach.

For illustrative purposes, however, I had a pretty nasty fever this weekend, and it was stark how much more difficult to be virtuous it was when my body felt so horrible. At the same time, however, it forced me to slow down some, to really stop and reflect on things in a new way. One thing that I realized is just what I said; it is immensely more difficult to be virtuous when my body feels horrible. Some might see this as an excuse; my body feels horrible, therefore practicing virtue is going to be hard, but the reality is that we actually have a substantial amount of ability to change how our body feels. This is why I keep telling people to exercise and eat healthy. In the process, you will need to build some discipline, and in doing so your body will begin to feel better overall, creating a sort of feedback loop, where the practice of virtue leads to easier practice of another, which leads to easier practice of another, until they start to become truly habitual responses to various external stimuli. Next thing you know, you’re a saint! I jest of course, but this feedback loop is seriously beneficial, despite being rather difficult to attain.

One thing that has helped me immensely in my own practice of gratitude is cigars. Not for everyone, I know, but the underlying idea is transferable. Cigars beget stillness. They require 45 to 90 mins of stillness. For me and many others, they represent a healthy solitude, to sit, to appreciate, and to fully exist in the moment. No distraction, just stillness. I have not found any other more effective means for generating gratitude in my life than lighting up a cigar, sitting out on my porch, watching the birds, and the sunset, and the smoke billowing away into the sky. All I can ever seem to do in those moments is thank God for an incomprehensibly good existence.

The point is this; find something that forces you to sit still for 1 hour a week. Sit somewhere beautiful, whether in nature, or somewhere with beautiful architecture. Do not even think about going on your phone. Seriously. Leave your phone in the house, or in your car, or something. Don’t bring a book, don’t go with someone else, just sit and watch the world around you. Perhaps this method won’t work for others, I don’t know, but these are the moments where my gratitude for life naturally bubbles out of me. I suppose calling it natural when it requires this much intentional effort may be a bit of a stretch, but I digress.

Gratitude is a choice, and it is a choice that is so often drowned among the waves of consumeristic distraction that wash over us on the daily. Fighting these waves is not easy, but it is necessary if we wish to advance forward, and heal from the cultural decay that rots away all around us. Each of us has the ability to choose to be thankful for that which we are blessed with. Do so, and do so often. Thanks for reading. As always, let me know what you think.
