Ideas.

Jan 12, 2026

One thing that always stands out to me when I am talking with people who have been raised in the west is how few people understand that ideas come from somewhere, and conversely, lead somewhere. Perhaps it is a side effect of the common secular, syncretistic attitudes towards belief systems, or in other words, the current cultural value that claims there is no inconsistency between belief systems, and that adopting, or ‘integrating’ small pieces from various worldviews is completely unproblematic and perhaps even helpful. A closer examination of this worldview renders it moot rather quickly, but most people never critically examine their belief systems.

At some point in the west we began to convince ourselves that these syncretism’s had no consequences, that importing ideas (and people) from the far corners of the earth on mass was actually a good thing. It has become quite clear in recent years that this failed experiment is on its last legs. This is due to one simple fact; ideas have an origin, and ideas have consequences. The idea that all cultures are equally moral comes from somewhere, and it leads somewhere. This is important to realize because the ideas that have allowed us to succeed as a people are intimately interwoven in our history, and failing to recognize and be grateful for those ideas in the last 70 years or so has led us all down a dark path.

This is most clearly the case in the United States of America. At its founding, the US was a nation of protestant Christian peoples, organized according to Christian ideas and law based on those ideas, and living in harmony as such. Whether or not you want to call it a Christian nation or not is irrelevant, the facts remain as such. Nearly everyone participating in this new American experiment fit those qualifications. Some of the founders were deists, sure, but even they were thoroughly immersed in the Christian worldview. The ideas that led to the American founding were almost universally Christian ideas. They came from somewhere, and they were pointed towards something (namely Christ).

This is significant because in recent years we have divorced these ideas from their only logical source. Simply put, many of the ideas that you, reading this substack, have always understood to be true and good, only exist because of Jesus Christ. This remains true even if you do not believe it. These ideas work solely because they are rooted in the character of a good God, and His divine intention for us humans. Divorcing the ideas from their origin is what got us into this mess in the first place. We have treated ideas as independent and interchangeable, and allowed ourselves the fantasy that there exists no consequence for borrowing a few ideas from secularism from time to time (for example). We have also treated people who adhere to these differing ideas as though there is no difference between them, although that is a topic for another time.

This brings up an interesting point however; can we profit from adopting good ideas, whose origin we are in contention with. In other words, can one benefit from the prosperity afforded to a nation that teaches and uplifts good ideas, while also rejecting the God from whom those ideas flow forth? Yes and no. There is a very strong case to be made that you cannot have your cake and eat it too. Some may argue that a nation under God is nothing if its individual citizenry are not followers of Christ. I agree and disagree. I think a nation that aligns itself with goodness and truth will be better off than a nation which doesn’t, simply because goodness and truth naturally lead to prosperity in the long run. That being said, true Christianity is better than cultural Christianity. But to be fair, cultural Christianity is far better than whatever garbage we have now. At least it would be somewhat logically consistent. As it stands, many people greatly value certain Christian ideas, while simultaneously valuing ideas that are nothing less than satanic.

Abortion would be a good example of this. Folks who are in favour of abortion generally hold the idea that all people are valuable and of worth, they simply have convinced themselves that those in utero are not yet people. They also tend to be not Christians. This is interesting because the idea that all people are valuable and of worth, is a Christian idea. It came from somewhere, and it is pointed towards something. In other words, the folks who seem to be almost always in direct opposition to Christianity operate on the assumption that parts of it are true, without knowing. But they do this, while rejecting other Christian ideas, such as the value of human life (beginning at conception).

So why does any of this really matter? Is it really the end of the world that people live according to shopping cart worldviews, where they pick and choose beliefs as they please? It does, because ideas are not just ingredients on a shelf. They have an origin, and consequences, and they interact with and sometimes combat one another, and suggesting that syncretism actually makes any sense is to deny the very existence of truth. At the end of the day, half-truths are still just lies. Eve was deceived in the garden by a collection of half-truths, Satan loves to use truth, but truth distorted, just enough that it becomes a weapon.

Our human societies succeed to the degree to which they are properly pointed at that which is good, and that which is true (and that which is beautiful, but I digress). As long as we insist on arbitrating according to our own syncretism’s, instead of according to the fount of truth Himself, we will fail. We will fall. We will suffer. It’s time for a return to form. It’s time to return to a world where we acknowledge exactly where the ideas we value come from, and where they are going.
