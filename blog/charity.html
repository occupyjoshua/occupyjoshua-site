Charity.

Feb 2, 2026

So this week I want to discuss the virtue of charity. Not the modern definition though, I am not referring to the practice of giving things away to those in need (a topic for another time), and I am also not referring to what we would today simply refer to as love. Rather I am interested in the practice of giving other people the benefit of the doubt. I suspect that if this script were ever to reach a substantial western population (doubtful it seems), I would promptly find myself accosted by many people refusing to give me any charity (that is, the benefit of the doubt). Since there will likely be both misunderstanding, and misrepresentation, allow me to define precisely what I am not advocating for; I am not advocating for a naïve, unlearned, careless approach to interacting with other people. I am not saying we should all just act as though everyone’s opinions are equal and valid and have merit. This is clearly not the case, and much of online rhetoric at the moment is people coming to terms with the fact that not all ideas are created equal. This is a good thing.

What I am saying, however, is that people on the internet need to relearn charity, that is, the ability to objectively look at what someone is saying (and not saying), and evaluate their words on the basis of the their merits, in a rational manner, without excessive emotion, and in a manner which fairly (or charitably) characterizes their statements and beliefs. If you knew me, you would know very clearly that I am thoroughly uninterested in the post-modern assumption that all ideas are equal and interchangeable. I understand that modernity has its issues when dealing with the subjective (yet very real) aspects of life, but post-modernist thinking today tends to swing the pendulum way too far in the opposite direction. Objective reality exists outside of our perception of it, and acting as though it doesn’t helps no one. This means that there are ideas which bear more merit than others, as they align more properly with truth.

That being said, there is a reason Christ instructed us to speak truth, in love. There is a reason why so much of the fruit of the spirit consists of attributes related to our treatment of others. The way we treat others matters, and at the moment, especially online, we treat others in a thoroughly uncharitable manner. Perhaps an example is in order.

A few weeks ago a well known Twitch streamer known as Pokimane released a short video talking about her interest in celibacy (something I personally practice, something the church advocates for, and something that is truly in line with God’s design for humanity, by the way), and to say the responses lacked charity would be a rather egregious understatement. The comments on twitter were nothing short of deplorable, calling her every manner of insult in the book, but they all shared one simple thing in common; every one of them made far sweeping assumptions about her, regarding things they could never actually know about her, and used those assumptions to characterize her in the most negative way possible. Put simply, every one of these folks lied, to themselves, and to each other, and used those lies as fuel to attack her.

This is true, even if the things they assumed were true. Why? Because none of them have any actual clue what they are talking about. It is entirely reasonable to assume she has been acting in this manner for some time, and even if she hasn’t, this sort of rhetoric is still rooted in falsehoods. Even if she had been sleeping with half of Los Angeles, the people calling her a whore would still be in the wrong. Just because you guessed right doesn’t mean you weren’t lying when you said it.

This woman is not perfect, but in my estimation, she is among the most uncharitably treated people on the internet. There are countless other examples, of her doing or saying something rather innocuous, sometimes even outright positive (as in this case), and the internet lies about the situation and bullies her incessantly. Make no mistake, assuming things about people in this manner, and using those assumptions as the basis for a hateful comment, or video on the internet is evil. This is just bearing false witness against someone. It is a defiance of the ninth commandment. That’s all it is. I understand this won’t carry weight for many people, but let’s call a spade a spade.

So what is the alternative? It’s quite simple really; tell the truth. If you don’t know what the truth is, don’t make stuff up. Don’t make far reaching assumptions based on insufficient evidence. Don’t bully people on the internet (even if you don’t like them), rather critically examine things they actually say and do, and only worry about things you can actually verify. There is enough deception in our world today, we don’t need to contribute even further to false narratives. Each of us would do well to remember that we will all stand before God one day and be held to account for the things we said and did. That won’t be a comfortable conversation for any of us, but it will be made even more uncomfortable if we consistently practice lying about people to ourselves, and others.

Give each other the benefit of the doubt. Practice the golden rule; ‘Do unto others, as you would have them do unto you.’ Stop judging people without sufficient reason to, especially on the basis of a single headline, or a few comments on the internet. This is one practical way we can all change the world for the better, and it requires almost nothing from us.
